🌷 Understand Your Cycle

🩸

Menstrual Phase

This phase begins on the first day of your period. You may experience bleeding, cramps, tiredness or changes in mood. Experiences vary from person to person.

🌱

Follicular Phase

This phase starts on the first day of menstruation and continues until ovulation. Hormonal changes support the development of a follicle in the ovary.

🥚

Ovulation

Ovulation is when an ovary releases an egg. Its timing varies, so a calendar estimate cannot confirm the exact ovulation date.

🌙

Luteal Phase

After ovulation, hormone levels change as the body prepares for a possible pregnancy. Some people notice premenstrual symptoms during this phase.

📝

Track Your Symptoms

Record cramps, headaches, mood changes, energy levels and other symptoms. Keeping a record may help you recognise patterns over time.

📅

Cycle Regularity

Compare the start dates of your periods over several cycles. Cycle length can vary, and one unusual cycle does not automatically mean there is a problem.

💗 Your Wellness Journey

Small, consistent habits can help you care for yourself. Keep a record of your period dates, notice how you feel, rest when needed and seek medical advice if you have symptoms that concern you.

🌼 Everyday Self-Care Tips

💧

Stay Hydrated

Drink water regularly throughout the day. Adjust your fluid intake to your activity and needs.

🥗

Eat Balanced Meals

Include a variety of vegetables, fruits, whole grains and protein-rich foods in your meals.

😴

Get Enough Rest

Maintain a regular sleep routine and give yourself time to rest when you feel tired.

🚶‍♀️

Stay Active

Gentle walking, stretching or other comfortable physical activity may help support well-being.

🧘‍♀️

Manage Stress

Try relaxing activities such as deep breathing, meditation, journaling or listening to music.

📔

Keep a Cycle Journal

Record period dates, symptoms and changes in your daily routine to discuss with a healthcare provider when necessary.

⚕️ Important health information

This page provides general educational information and does not diagnose medical conditions.
